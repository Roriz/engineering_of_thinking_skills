# GEO company discovery

Turn the caller's materials and a probing interview into a usable company knowledge pack for GEO audits, content planning, and evidence-backed writing. Capture how the company actually works, who buys, what customers need to know, and which claims can be supported. Do not turn aspirations or marketing language into established facts.

This skill borrows the dependency-aware questioning of grilling, but is self-contained. Build and update documents during the interview; do not require an exhaustive interview or a ceremonial approval before saving drafts.

## Start from available evidence

1. Read supplied materials and existing company documents before asking questions they already answer. Accept text, decks, PDFs, screenshots, transcripts, research, sales material, case studies, support questions, and website links. Use suitable read-only tools; inspect screenshots visually and identify coverage limits. Record inaccessible or partially parsed inputs instead of pretending they were fully reviewed.
2. Identify the company, requested use, desired audience/language, and output location from context. Ask only material unresolved questions. This is company discovery, not a request to audit every URL or conduct broad market research. Inspect supplied links; use additional public research only when requested or necessary to resolve a material external fact. Prefer primary sources and record scope/date. Do not infer an industry statistic from generic model knowledge.
3. Create a compact source inventory and a draft gap map: what the inputs establish, what they merely assert, what conflicts, and which important questions remain. Old presentations, current product pages, and interview recollections can disagree. Keep the discrepancy visible until evidence or a responsible source resolves it; newest is not automatically authoritative.

Read [references/document-map.md](references/document-map.md) when planning the pack. Read [references/evidence-rules.md](references/evidence-rules.md) before recording claims. Create only documents useful to this company and the caller's scope; do not populate an empty catalog with invented detail.

## Interview in focused rounds

Read [references/interview-playbook.md](references/interview-playbook.md) before the first round. Use it as a question bank, not a questionnaire to exhaust.

- Ask the highest-value questions whose prerequisites are already understood. Typically use one to three questions per round; let the user request a longer batch. Briefly explain what each answer will resolve. Wait for answers before asking questions that depend on them.
- Probe vague claims with specific examples, actual buying situations, exclusions, dates, and evidence. Ask follow-ups that expose contradictions without being combative. Adapt to the business; do not impose SaaS, enterprise procurement, or a founder-led model on everyone.
- Separate factual questions from decisions. Recommend options for a positioning or scope decision when helpful, but never supply a "recommended" revenue figure, customer profile, market size, credential, client result, or other purported company fact.
- Use known material to reduce the user's work: show the current understanding and ask them to resolve the consequential gap rather than repeat a broad onboarding interview. Do not require the caller to retrieve facts already accessible in their supplied materials.
- Let the user answer partially, skip, or say "unknown." Record unresolved items and continue on independent areas. If the user requests document-only synthesis or no interview, draft from the evidence and record questions without demanding answers. Do not manufacture answers after a timeout.

Maintain a small resumable interview state in the pack index: answered decisions, material conflicts/gaps, current round, and next useful questions. Preserve a concise account of relevant answers with date and source; do not copy the entire conversation or unrelated private details.

## Write the knowledge pack as it develops

Use the requested destination. Otherwise use `company-knowledge/<company-slug>/` in the active task workspace; if identity is still unknown, hold this choice until the first answer or use a clearly temporary discovery folder. Do not write the caller's company material into the skill installation or the Ranqia guide folder. Inspect existing files before updating; preserve unrelated material and distinguish corrections from unresolved alternatives.

Start with the evidence already available and update the affected documents after interview rounds. Use the caller's language, clear Markdown, and factual prose. Mark documents as draft, reviewed by the caller, or otherwise qualified according to what actually happened. A user statement remains user-reported even when the caller reviews the document. Link factual claims to source IDs and locators; keep interpretation and proposed positioning visibly separate.

The usual pack covers company/brand identity, offerings, real customers and proposed audiences, market/industry context, positioning/alternatives, proof/claims, and audience questions. The [document map](references/document-map.md) defines the boundaries and handoff. Merge thin topics or omit irrelevant ones rather than duplicating facts. Preserve canonical facts in one place and cross-link them.

Develop only evidence-backed assets: for example, a factual brand brief, offering fact sheet, buyer-question map, comparison-criteria brief, or case-study outline. An outline awaiting customer results is not a finished case study. Distinguish observed customer questions from questions synthesized for future validation; neither is automatically a measured AI fan-out.

## Stop and hand off usefully

Stop interviewing when the agreed core questions are adequately covered for the intended task, the user asks to stop or draft, or remaining gaps are explicitly deferred and do not prevent a useful draft. Do not claim full verification or completeness merely because questioning stopped.

Finish with links to created/updated files, a short account of what is usable now, and the consequential unresolved facts or decisions. Explain what evidence would resolve them. Claim substantiation, factual ownership, and permission to disclose are separate considerations: internal documentation does not grant permission to publish client names, testimonials, private prices, or confidential source material.

The pack can be supplied to a GEO website audit or content-planning task as business context. It is not proof of model visibility, customer demand, independent reputation, or causality. The supplied Ranqia guides motivate clear entities, intent-specific customer questions, reliable claims, and source fit; they supply no facts about the caller's company. Another skill is not required to complete this workflow. Save drafts and reports only; do not publish, change the website, or contact customers or third parties unless separately instructed.
