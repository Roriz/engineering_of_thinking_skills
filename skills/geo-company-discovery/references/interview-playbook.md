# Interview playbook

Choose questions by their effect on the next document or decision. Gather examples and counterexamples; do not ask every question below or demand exact numbers the company does not track.

## Plan the next round

Track dependencies. Offerings and the intended market usually precede detailed buyer prompts; verified capability and real customer evidence precede outcome claims; actual alternatives precede competitor positioning. Independent branches can advance while a disputed figure stays open.

Open with a concise synthesis of what the material already says. Ask about the most consequential unknown or conflict. A useful round looks like:

> I can describe the product from the spec, but the deck and sales notes disagree about who buys it.
>
> **Q1 — Actual customer:** Which customer type has bought it recently? Walk me through one deal: situation, decision-maker, chosen offering, and reason for choosing it. An anonymized example is enough.
>
> **Q2 — Product boundary:** The deck says X while the spec says Y. Which applies today, and is there a version, date, or product owner that settles it?

Do not answer Q1 on the user's behalf. If proposing a positioning direction, label it as a recommendation to consider rather than the market's proven view.

## Identity and offerings

Use when names, scope, current availability, or product boundaries are unclear.

- What are the trading name, legal company, product brands, and significant naming variants? Which relationships are documented?
- What can a customer buy today? What is a service, a product, a package, a reseller offering, or a future plan?
- Show one real delivery or use case from start to finish. What must the buyer already have, and what does the company actually provide?
- What does the offering not do? Who is a poor fit, and which promises should the team avoid?
- Which markets, languages, service areas, channels, and pricing terms apply? Which are internal, variable, outdated, or not yet settled?

Specificity beats category slogans. “An AI platform for everyone” needs a concrete task and boundary, not a more polished version of the same slogan.

## Clients, customers, and buying behavior

Keep real customer history separate from intended audience and ICP hypotheses. Do not invent personas or demographic details to fill a template.

- Describe a recent win, a loss, and a poor-fit inquiry if available. What triggered the search and what would have happened without the purchase?
- Who uses, pays, influences, approves, or blocks the purchase? In a consumer business some of these may be the same person.
- What did the buyer ask before committing? Is that wording in a call, email, support ticket, search log, or the interviewee's recollection?
- What alternatives were considered: direct competitor, manual work, spreadsheet, internal team, or doing nothing? Why was each plausible?
- What constraints mattered: price, integration, trust, eligibility, geography, time, capacity, risk, or another factor?

Ask for anonymized examples when names are unnecessary. Record whether client-name or testimonial use is permitted only when relevant to a public asset; do not make disclosure approval a prerequisite for documenting an anonymized internal case.

## Market and industry

Separate what the company believes from what independent evidence establishes.

- Which category do buyers use, and is that different from the company's preferred label?
- What adjacent categories are commonly confused with the offering? Which geographic or regulatory differences change suitability?
- What market assertion materially affects positioning or prioritization? Which source, date, geography, segment, and definition support it?
- What changed recently in customer behavior or the industry? Is it observed in deals, supported by external research, or a hypothesis?

Challenge terms such as “fastest growing,” “market leader,” and “no competitors.” Do not demand a market-size calculation if it does not affect the intended task. If external validation is unavailable, retain the belief as such rather than inventing a total addressable market.

## Differentiation and proof

Turn attractive claims into bounded, reviewable statements.

- Compared with which alternative, for which buyer and task, is this advantage meaningful?
- What concrete capability or evidence supports it? Where does the advantage disappear or reverse?
- For a result: what was measured, for whom, over what period, with what baseline and denominator? Was it typical, selected, estimated, or a single case?
- Can someone trace the statement back to the source? What caveats would change its meaning?
- Which claim appears in marketing today but the team would hesitate to defend? What can accurately replace it?

A customer's outcome does not by itself establish that the product caused it. The interview should identify the evidence gap, not talk the caller into a stronger claim.

## GEO questions and useful assets

After the business is understood, translate it into useful questions:

- What does a buyer need to understand, compare, verify, or locate before choosing?
- Which product or company facts answer those questions? Which require independent corroboration, a calculator, a comparison, or original research?
- What did real buyers ask, and what additional questions are hypotheses for validation?
- Which source can legitimately establish the answer: official terms, specifications, independent research, customer evidence, or something else?

Cover different intents and entity levels when relevant: best-fit offering, price, alternatives, trust, local availability, and where to buy. This is an audience-question map, not a claim about actual prompts used at scale or model citation behavior.

## Handling stalled or conflicting answers

If the caller cannot answer, record the unknown and a useful next step; do not repeat the same demand. For conflicts, preserve both sources and ask the responsible factual question. For a preference, let the user choose. If a question is sensitive or declined, continue with a scoped or anonymized document. Save draft progress before ending an interview turn, and carry forward only the unresolved questions that still matter.
