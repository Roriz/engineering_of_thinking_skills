# Blog and long-form review

Use for articles, explainers, comparisons, research pieces, and long-form social articles. Establish the intended job; do not assess a first-person opinion essay as though it were a product specification.

## Review the reading journey

- **Title and opening:** Does the promised subject match the body? Can the intended reader identify the question and main answer without excessive setup? Opinions may use a narrative opening; flag it only when it obstructs the intended job.
- **Sections and coverage:** Do headings organize distinct questions, criteria, examples, or evidence? Address meaningful missing subtopics from the company question map, not guessed search volume. A comparison should explain applicability and trade-offs rather than invent one universal winner.
- **Passage usefulness:** Are key answers understandable with their qualifications and evidence? Avoid treating quotability as a requirement for every paragraph or suggesting repetitive definitions and keywords.
- **Sources and original value:** Inspect the actual support for numbers and important assertions. Distinguish research findings, expert interpretation, product facts, and customer anecdotes. Explain why a source fits the question and whether essential scope is missing.
- **Time and authorship:** Dates/methodology and relevant expertise matter when they affect interpretation. Do not demand cosmetic freshness or a credential unrelated to the subject. A byline does not validate the claim.
- **Related information:** Check supplied or relevant linked fact sheets, terms, methodology, and supporting pages. Recommend useful connections rather than link quotas. Do not claim a destination is absent from the whole website after seeing one article.

## Format and technical scope

Draft text supports editorial judgments but cannot establish delivered HTML, structured data, live indexability, page speed, or actual crawler access. A screenshot supports only visible coverage. If live HTML is inspected, a meaningful heading/link issue may be noted, but do not expand a content audit into a whole-site technical review. Broader site investigation belongs in a separately scoped website audit.

No mandatory FAQ, schema, minimum article length, semantic domain, competitor list, or language expansion follows from this review. Recommend the smallest content change that answers the observed need. Keep unsupported topic-expansion ideas separate from confirmed deficiencies.
